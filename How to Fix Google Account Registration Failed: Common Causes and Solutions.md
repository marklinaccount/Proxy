When creating a Google account, what should you do if your phone number cannot be verified, you do not receive a verification code, or the page repeatedly says that your account cannot be created? Different error messages can have different causes, and repeatedly submitting information or switching networks may not solve the problem.

This article covers common Google account registration issues, explains their main causes, and provides practical solutions to help you identify the problem and find the right way to resolve it.

**I. Common Reasons Why Google Account Registration Fails**

Google account registration failures are usually caused by multiple factors rather than a single issue. Understanding the specific cause helps you troubleshoot more effectively and avoid triggering additional verification checks through repeated attempts.

1. Network and Proxy Environment Issues

When creating a Google account, an unstable network connection, frequent proxy node changes, or an IP address with a history of suspicious access may increase verification difficulty. This is particularly relevant when using shared proxies, where multiple users share the same IP address. 

Suspicious activity by other users may also affect the IP address'soverall reputation.

2. Phone Number Verification Failure

Phone number verification is a common obstacle during Google account registration. Using a mainland China phone number with the +86 country code may encounter restrictions in certain situations.

- The page says, "This phone number cannot be used for verification." This may happen when the number has been used for verification too
  many times, triggering Google's risk control measures.
- No SMS verification code arrives. This may be caused by carrier delays or problems receiving text messages.

Note that even if the phone number itself is valid, a high-risk outgoing IP address may still prevent the verification code from being delivered.

3. Browser Environment Issues

Using domestic browsers such as 360 Browser or QQ Browser, which may have built-in review or filtering mechanisms, or having browser cache and language settings that reveal your actual location, can interfere with the registration process.

For example, residual Chinese language preferences and historical cookies in Chrome that do not match the location of the current proxy IP may create inconsistencies in the browsing environment. Google may interpret these inconsistencies as signs of an unusual access environment.

2. Adjust Your Browser Language Settings

If your browser language differs significantly from your actual usage environment, try adjusting the language settings to reduce unnecessary inconsistencies.

Recommended steps: Open Chrome and go to Settings → Languages. Add English and move it to an appropriate priority level. Restart the browser and try registering again.

3. Use Incognito Mode

Browser cache and cookies may interfere with the registration process. Incognito mode reduces the impact of existing cookies and some browsing data, allowing you to open the registration page in a session without your usual browsing history and cached data. This may make the session appear more like a fresh browsing environment and reduce the likelihood of triggering Google's risk control measures.

Recommended steps: Open the menu in the top-right corner of Chrome, select "New Incognito Window," and then visit the Google Account registration page to complete the process.

4. Try a Different Registration Method

Verification procedures vary across devices and registration methods. Registration through the desktop website may involve stricter risk controls, while mobile registration may be less restrictive. Some users report that the mobile registration process is simpler than the desktop version. If another Google account is already signed in on the phone, creating a new account may sometimes allow you to skip phone verification.

Recommended steps: Open the Gmail app on your phone or use Google's official account registration page on your mobile device. Follow the on-screen instructions, and avoid repeatedly switching devices or submitting registration requests in quick succession.

**III. Additional Tips for Registering a Google Account**

Resolving registration issues is only the first step. To reduce the risk
of future login problems and make account recovery easier, keep the
following points in mind.

1. Wait Before Trying Again After Repeated Failures

If registration has failed several times in a row, avoid repeatedly
submitting requests from the same device and IP address. Repeated
attempts may reinforce risk signals. Stop trying and wait 24--48 hours
before attempting registration again using a stable network and device.

During this period, avoid rapidly switching phone numbers, IP addresses,
or devices to make repeated attempts, as this may increase verification
difficulty.

2. Add a Recovery Email After Registration

After creating a Google account, consider linking a non-Google email
address that you use regularly and can access reliably, such as QQ Mail,
NetEase Mail, or Outlook.

If your account is flagged for a login from an unfamiliar location or
becomes locked, a recovery email may provide a straightforward way to
recover the account or resolve access restrictions.

3. Avoid Frequent Device and Network Changes Immediately After
   Registration

The first 3--7 days after account creation can be a sensitive period for
a new account. Try to sign in and use the account on the same device and
network node used during registration. Avoid frequently signing out,
clearing browser data, or switching repeatedly between your phone and
computer.

Frequent changes to the browsing environment may trigger additional
verification checks and, in severe cases, lead to restrictions on the
new account.

**IV. FAQ**

1. Can I Switch Back to My Regular Network Immediately After
   Registration?

It is not recommended. During the initial monitoring period, a new
account may be sensitive to changes in its access environment. Switching
abruptly from a residential IP to a data center IP or frequently
changing locations may trigger additional verification.

It is better to maintain a stable environment for a while before making
adjustments based on your actual needs.

2. What Should I Do If I Never Receive the Google Registration
   Verification Code?

First, confirm that your phone number and country or region code are
correct. Then check your phone signal and SMS reception. If Google
offers alternative verification methods, such as voice verification, you
can follow the on-screen instructions to try them. Available
verification methods may vary by account and region.

3. Can I Create a Google Account Without a Gmail Address?

Yes. Google allows users to create a Google account using an existing
non-Gmail email address in certain registration flows, provided they
verify the email as required.

Keep in mind that using an existing email address to create a Google
account does not create a new Gmail address. For more information, refer
to Google's official account creation guide.

**V. Conclusion**

Common reasons for Google account registration failure include network
and IP environment issues, phone number verification restrictions, and
unsuitable browser settings or cached data. Configuring a clean
residential proxy and maintaining a consistent outgoing IP environment,
along with adjusting the browser language, using an Incognito window,
and choosing an appropriate registration method, may help resolve many
common verification issues.

After successfully registering, remember to add a recovery email and
recovery phone number where available, and maintain normal account usage
habits to help keep your account accessible and stable.
